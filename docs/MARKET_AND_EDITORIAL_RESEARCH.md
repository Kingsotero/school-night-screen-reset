# Market and editorial research

## Comparable titles

1. *The Anxious Generation* (Jonathan Haidt, 2024) frames the cultural problem at large scale.
2. *The Screentime Solution* (Emily Cherkin, 2024) uses a judgment-free, tech-intentional approach.
3. *Technology's Child* (Katie Davis, 2023/2024) differentiates needs by developmental stage.
4. *Power On* (Ash Brandin, 2025) argues for a calmer, less moralized relationship with screens.
5. *The Tech Exit* (Clare Morell, 2025) advocates a more restrictive family-level response.
6. *Screen Smart Children* (Mark A. Williams and Gavin McCormack) combines explanation with practical tools.
7. *A Kids Book About Technology* (Amber Case, 2025) makes the conversation accessible to ages 5-9.
8. *What's My Tween Thinking?* (Tanith Carey and Angharad Rudkin, 2024) organizes guidance around real scenarios and age bands.
9. *Raising a Screen-Smart Kid* (Julianna Miner, 2019) takes a facts-over-fear approach.
10. *Screen-Free Fun* (Shannon Philpott-Sanders, 2017) focuses on replacement activities.

## White space

Most comparable books require hours of reading before implementation. Marketplace printables tend to offer a chart, contract, or activity list without a decision sequence. The product differentiates through:

- a ten-minute start;
- one boundary at a time;
- exact language for common moments;
- separate routines for ages 6-8 and 9-12;
- a seven-night observe-adjust loop;
- repair guidance after a difficult night;
- an explicit distinction between recreational and necessary screen use.

## Evidence base used in the product

- The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends a family media plan that accounts for a child's developmental stage, sets consistent rules, and supports open communication: https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/How-to-Make-a-Family-Media-Use-Plan.aspx
- The AAP's current public guidance emphasizes the 5 Cs, adult modeling, media-free spaces, and family discussion rather than a universal one-number rule: https://www.healthychildren.org/English/news/Pages/creating-a-child-friendly-digital-world-AAP-releases-new-media-recommendations.aspx
- Pew's 2025 parent survey found that managing screen time is a priority for most parents of children 12 and under, while 42% said they could be doing better: https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/10/08/how-parents-manage-screen-time-for-kids/
- Singapore's 2025 Digital Parenting Survey found that only 37% of parents felt confident guiding digital habits; limited time and children's reluctance were common barriers: https://www.mddi.gov.sg/newsroom/mddi-study-shows-most-parents-guide-children-s-digital-use-but-would-like-more-support/

## Editorial choices

- Do not use a fear-first frame.
- Do not label a child as addicted.
- Do not prescribe a universal daily hour limit for ages 6-12.
- Treat schoolwork, communication, accessibility, and creative use separately from recreational use.
- Encourage professional support when screen conflict is paired with safety concerns, severe distress, withdrawal from daily life, or persistent family disruption.
