# Editorial quality report

## Scope

This review covers the complete 29-page English edition and the complete 29-page Spanish edition. It evaluates the kit as a paid action product, not as a general-interest parenting book. The editorial target is a parent who is already tired, may open the file in the middle of a difficult week, and needs to act before reading every page.

## Beta-reader simulation

### Reader 1: The devourer

- **What keeps this reader moving:** page 2 starts with the specific 7:42 p.m. scene and gives a ten-minute setup; pages 9-15 create a visible seven-night path; the scripts on pages 16-19 remove the hardest writing work.
- **Probable pause point:** page 4, if the baseline worksheet feels like homework before action.
- **Why the pause is survivable:** page 2 explicitly lets the reader begin tonight with one rule, one warning, and two alternatives. The worksheet is useful, but not a gate.
- **Top strengths:** immediate start; short nightly actions; reusable scripts and printables.
- **Missing element:** a dramatic before-and-after story. It was intentionally excluded because it would lengthen the kit and invite an outcome claim the product cannot guarantee.
- **Recommendation:** buy/use. The product solves a narrow job quickly.

### Reader 2: The critic

- **What earns trust:** the kit separates recreational use from homework, communication, accessibility, and creative work; it does not prescribe a universal hour limit; the support page states when a printable plan is not enough.
- **Probable objection:** the phrase “calmer transitions” in the first subtitle implied an emotional result beyond the buyer's control.
- **Correction made:** changed the English promise to “more predictable transitions.”
- **Second objection:** the first Night 6 draft stated that children cooperate better when given ownership, without evidence inside the product.
- **Correction made:** turned the claim into a home experiment: test one small, age-appropriate job and observe whether it helps.
- **Top strengths:** narrow scope; transparent limitations; source notes without pretending that an organization endorses the product.
- **Recommendation:** use, with the non-clinical boundary preserved in marketing.

### Reader 3: The hostile reader

- **Likely attack:** “This is generic AI parenting advice padded into a PDF.”
- **Where the product answers it:** the visible-rule formula on page 6; the seven-minute meeting on page 8; distinct routines for ages 6-8 and 9-12 on pages 20-21; device-parking labels, agreement, tracker, troubleshooting table, and repair sequence on pages 22-27.
- **Probable abandonment point:** if mixed-language labels or translated jargon make the editions feel mass-produced.
- **Corrections made:** removed bilingual interface labels from each monolingual edition; localized the Spanish cover title; replaced “piece of ownership” with “one small job”; corrected the unnatural Spanish sentence about a 7:30 endpoint.
- **Remaining vulnerability:** the kit cannot make a child comply. The copy therefore promises a clearer plan and more predictable handoffs, not obedience.
- **Recommendation:** credible for the stated $12 low-ticket position, provided the sales page avoids fake urgency and unqualified clinical claims.

## Prose and humanization pass

- No em dashes, “revolutionary,” “game-changing,” “science-backed,” “proven,” or similar inflated language remains.
- Instructions favor concrete nouns and observable actions: timer, parking place, warning, endpoint, next activity, tracker.
- Repetitive parenting jargon was reduced. “Ownership” was replaced where plain “job” or “task” was clearer.
- The recurring line “A reset is a return to the plan, not a punishment” is retained as a deliberate memory device, not filler.
- English and Spanish are localized independently. The Spanish edition uses *reinicio*, not the borrowed *reset*, in its core identity.

## Production-prep checks

- Same functional sequence and page count in both editions: 29 pages.
- Page types, nightly sequence, source notes, disclaimers, and support boundaries are aligned.
- No universal screen-time limit, diagnosis, treatment instruction, or promised change in sleep, grades, mood, or behavior.
- Source URLs are displayed in full and are linked in the PDF.
- A4 page size, print-safe margins, page numbers, and household-use notice are present.
- Worksheets leave writable space; key instructions do not depend on color alone.

## Post-launch questions worth testing

1. Do buyers begin with page 2 or jump directly to the scripts?
2. Does “school-night” outperform “bedtime” in acquisition copy without narrowing the audience too far?
3. Are parents of children ages 9-12 more responsive to the routine page or the pushback scripts?
4. Which Spanish-speaking market prefers *pantallas* versus a more device-specific headline?
