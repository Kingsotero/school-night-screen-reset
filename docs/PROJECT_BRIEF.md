# Project brief

## Product

**Title:** The 7-Day School-Night Screen Reset

**Format:** a printable, action-first PDF toolkit in English and Spanish.

**Audience:** parents and caregivers of children ages 6-12 who want clearer, more consistent boundaries for recreational screens on school nights.

**Job to be done:** replace improvised nightly negotiations with one visible plan the family can understand, test, and adjust.

**Scope:** educational planning and communication. The product does not diagnose, treat, or promise changes in sleep, grades, behavior, addiction, or mental health.

## Positioning

The market is crowded with long books that explain why screens are a problem and thin printables that offer little beyond a tracker. This kit occupies the gap between them: it helps a tired parent decide what to do tonight, supplies the words to say, and creates a seven-night feedback loop.

## Voice

Warm, direct, calm, practical, non-judgmental. The writing assumes that screens can be useful and enjoyable. It does not shame parents or children, and it does not treat a difficult transition as a moral failure.

## Signature device

Every section ends with a concrete **Tonight** action. The recurring line is: **A reset is a return to the plan, not a punishment.**

## Offer architecture

- Core: seven-night implementation plan.
- Friction removers: scripts, age-adjusted routines, family agreement, replacement menu, tracker, troubleshooting map.
- First value: the parent can make one clear decision and use one script within ten minutes of opening the kit.
- Product price tested by the landing page: US$12.
- Guarantee language is intentionally absent until the seller chooses and can operationally honor a policy.
- Scarcity and countdowns are intentionally absent.

## Landing page

- Routes: `/en` and `/es`.
- Static-image-ad continuity: the first viewport must repeat the product name, seven-night frame, practical outcome, and visible PDF mockup.
- CTA is checkout-agnostic and points to a configurable placeholder anchor.
- The page must disclose digital delivery, printable A4 format, languages, audience, and non-clinical scope.

## Success criteria

- Both PDFs render without clipped text, missing glyphs, overlaps, or broken page numbers.
- The English and Spanish products contain the same functional value while sounding native in each language.
- A reader can take an action within ten minutes.
- The landing page builds successfully, renders at mobile and desktop widths, and gives each language its own metadata and canonical route.
